# Housing, Health and Deprivation in London

## Does where you live shape how long you live?

This project explores the relationship between deprivation, housing and health inequalities across London, and considers what the findings mean for affordable housing.

Created by Eila Hemmings.

## About the project

As a diagnostic radiography student with a strong interest in geography, I wanted to explore how the places people live connect to the health outcomes seen in clinical settings.

The project reviews public deprivation and life expectancy data, presents selected comparisons between London boroughs, and critically evaluates the limitations of the evidence.

## Research question

Do people in more deprived parts of London have worse health, and where does housing fit in?

## Approach

- Review the English Indices of Deprivation 2025 and published life expectancy figures.
- Explore how housing fits within the seven domains of deprivation.
- Compare selected London boroughs to illustrate differences in health outcomes.
- Evaluate what these comparisons can and cannot establish.
- Consider the relevance of the findings to affordable housing.

This is a presentation-based research project, not a coded statistical analysis of all London boroughs.

## Key findings presented

- Women's healthy life expectancy ranges from 57.0 years in Lewisham to 70.2 years in Richmond upon Thames—a difference of 13.2 years, using the 2021–2023 figures cited in the presentation.
- Two deprivation domains are particularly relevant to housing: Barriers to Housing and Services, and Living Environment.
- Housing should be considered alongside income, employment, education and access to services when exploring health inequalities.

## Critical evaluation

The presentation highlights several important limitations:

- Comparing the highest and lowest boroughs illustrates the size of the gap, but does not measure the relationship across every borough.
- Health is already a component of the Index of Multiple Deprivation, so the measures are not independent.
- Borough averages can hide substantial differences between neighbourhoods.
- Correlation does not establish causation, and these comparisons cannot isolate the effect of housing from other influences.

## Relevance to affordable housing

The project considers how place-based data can help identify areas of need and inform discussions about affordable housing.

It also reflects my interest in how housing associations, local authorities, investors and developers work together to respond to housing needs.
